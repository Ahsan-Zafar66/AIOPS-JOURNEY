answer of above question:
1. Public IP vs. Private IP
Public IP: This is the address that your router uses to communicate with the internet. It’s unique across the entire internet. Think of it as your home address; it tells the internet where to send data meant for you.

Private IP: These are addresses used within your local network (like your home Wi-Fi). They’re not unique on the internet. Think of them as apartment numbers in a building. Each device on your network gets its own private IP to communicate with other devices.

2. NAT (Network Address Translation)
NAT: This is a process used by routers to translate private IP addresses to a public IP address. When you send data from your device to the internet, NAT changes your private IP address to the public IP address of the router.

3. Router
Router: This device connects your local network (like your home) to the internet. It directs traffic between your devices and the internet, and it handles the NAT process.
Your Scenario
Wi-Fi Connection: When your PTCL service provider gives you a router, it assigns a public IP address to that router. Your router then assigns private IP addresses to each device connected to your Wi-Fi (like your laptop, mobile, and desktop).
Do all your devices get a different private IP or the same?
Different Private IPs: Each device will get a different private IP address assigned by the router. For example, your laptop might get 192.168.1.2, your mobile might get 192.168.1.3, and your desktop might get 192.168.1.4.

4. Gateway
Gateway: This refers to the router's IP address in your local network. It’s usually the first address in the subnet (e.g., 192.168.1.1). Your devices use this IP address to communicate with the router and, consequently, the internet.

5. How does the conversion from private IP to public IP happen?
Request to Google: When you search for "google.com" from your mobile:

Your device sends this request to the router using its private IP.
Router and NAT:

The router receives the request and uses NAT to change the private IP address of your mobile to its own public IP address.
The request now appears to come from the public IP address of the router.
Sending the Request: The router sends this request to Google. The request is now using the router's public IP.

Google's Response:

When Google sends data back (like the search results), it sends it to the router’s public IP address.
Router's Role Again:

The router receives the response and uses NAT to translate the public IP back to the private IP of your mobile device, allowing the correct device to receive the data.
Summary of Communication Process:
You search on Google using your mobile device’s private IP.
The router uses NAT to convert this private IP to its public IP.
The request goes to Google, which responds to the router’s public IP.
The router then converts it back to the mobile device’s private IP and sends the data to your mobile.
Conclusion
Every device on your local network gets a unique private IP.
The router has a public IP and handles the NAT process to manage communication between your devices and the internet.
The gateway is essentially the router’s address within your network.
